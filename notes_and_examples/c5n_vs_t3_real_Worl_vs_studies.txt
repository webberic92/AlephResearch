## ✅ Is `c5n.xlarge` a Good Example?

### `c5n.xlarge` Specs:

* **vCPUs**: 4
* **RAM**: 10.5 GiB
* **Network**: Up to 25 Gbps (enhanced networking)
* **Use case**: High-throughput compute tasks

### ✅ Why It’s Good for Benchmarking:

* Lets you **test peak performance ceiling**
* Useful for **stress testing** heavy transaction batches (like 1024 × 250B)
* Great for evaluating protocol **scalability and optimization**

### ⚠️ Why It’s *Not* Representative of Typical Nodes:

* It's **better than what most real blockchain nodes use**
* Most nodes (especially in permissionless settings) run on:

  * Less CPU (2 vCPUs or fewer)
  * Less RAM (2–8 GB)
  * Lower IOPS and bandwidth

---

## 🔎 Real-World Blockchain Node Specs

| Blockchain     | Node Type        | Typical Specs           | Notes                                      |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Ethereum**   | Full Node (Geth) | 2–4 vCPU, 8–16 GB RAM   | High disk usage for full sync              |
| **Bitcoin**    | Full Node (Core) | 1–2 vCPU, 2–8 GB RAM    | Modest RAM; needs SSD                      |
| **Solana**     | Validator        | 12–16 vCPU, 128+ GB RAM | Exceptionally high due to PoH + throughput |
| **Polkadot**   | Validator        | 4–8 vCPU, 8–32 GB RAM   | Higher RAM for WASM execution              |
| **Cosmos**     | Validator        | 2–4 vCPU, 4–16 GB RAM   | Mid-tier machines OK                       |
| **Tezos**      | Baking Node      | 2 vCPU, 8 GB RAM        | Modest RAM, active disk usage              |
| **Aleph Zero** | Authority Node   | 2–4 vCPU, 8–16 GB RAM   | Similar to Geth setup                      |

## 🧠 Implications for Your Work

### If your goal is:

* 🧪 **Protocol ceiling / optimization potential** → ✅ `c5n.xlarge` is **ideal**
* 🌍 **Real-world realism / cost-effectiveness** → ❌ `c5n.xlarge` is **too powerful**
* ⚖️ **Benchmarking RSA vs Merkle** → ✅ Good for fairness **if** both get equal resources

---

## 🔁 Recommendation

### Use `c5n.xlarge` for:

* Measuring **peak TPS**
* Stress-testing large batches (e.g. 1024 txs)
* Comparing protocol *scaling* limits

### Use `t3.medium` or `t3.large` for:

* Testing **realistic node constraints**
* Showing **where Merkle fails and RSA survives**
* Simulating public blockchain deployments

| Metric                      | `c5n.xlarge` Role                                    |
