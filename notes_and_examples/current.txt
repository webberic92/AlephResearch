Great question. Here's a direct comparison of which optimization would save the most — in terms of runtime performance, memory, and network overhead in your RSA accumulator-based Aleph ch-RBC protocol.
🔍 1. Embedding shard_primes in the Transaction struct

Purpose: Avoid recomputing hash_to_prime_128 during verification.

Cost savings:

    ✅ CPU savings: hash_to_prime_128 is expensive due to SHA-256 + Miller-Rabin primality test (even with caching).

    ❌ Network cost increases slightly: Transmitting additional ~16 bytes per prime, base64-encoded (~22 chars).

    ⚠️ Memory cost increases: Slightly larger DAG in memory.

Conclusion:
✅ Saves moderate CPU during prevote/commit phase.
Recommended if you are verifying thousands of transactions per round or suspect CPU bottlenecks.

    🧠 Reused Vec<BigInt> once per batch.

You already got most of the speed win here. ✅
🔍 3. Compact proof serialization (instead of base64)

Problem: Current base64 encoding of 256-bit proof = ~344 bits (43+ chars). That’s:

    ~44 bytes per proof per shard.

    Across 256 transactions and 4 data shards = ~45 KB extra per proposal message.

Improvement:

    Use raw bytes with binary serialization (e.g., bincode or prost) instead of base64.

    Or compress with zstd.

Conclusion:
✅ Big win on network size (~30–40% reduction in proof payload).
Especially valuable when scaling to high batch sizes (e.g., 131072 txs) or many shards.
